# Competitor teardown

## When to use
- A competitor is new to `brand/competitors.md` or shows up in deals more often
- Before a battlecard, comparison page or alternative page is written
- Twice a year for each main competitor

## Inputs
Ask the user for: the competitor, the specific questions the team has, and any internal notes (deal notes, call snippets).

Read: `brand/competitors.md`, `brand/company.md`, `brand/positioning.md`, `brand/claims-and-proof.md`, `brand/source-authority.md`.

## Steps
1. **Positioning.** Read the homepage, main product pages and "about" page. Record their headline, category claim, target audience and key messages, quoting the exact words with the date.
2. **Product.** List the main capabilities from their own documentation and release notes. Note recent releases (last 6-12 months) and anything announced but not yet available.
3. **Pricing and packaging.** Record list prices, editions, units (per user, per usage, flat) and any free tier, exactly as published, with currency and date. If pricing is not public, say so.
4. **Go-to-market.** Channels they invest in (events, paid search, content, community, partners), marketplace presence, partner ecosystem, sales motion (self-serve, sales-led, both).
5. **Customers.** Named customers and case studies; the industries and company sizes they cluster in.
6. **Reviews.** Read reviews on the main review sites from the last 12 months. Group into themes, count how many reviews support each theme, and quote two short examples per theme.
7. **Hiring.** Scan open roles. Note clusters (region, function, technology) that suggest where they are investing.
8. **Weak spots and strengths.** Separate sourced facts from inference. Label confidence.
9. **So what.** What it means for positioning, sales, content and paid search, and which agent should act.
10. **Proposed edits** to `brand/competitors.md`.

## Output template
```
# Teardown: <competitor> (as of <date>)
## Summary (5 bullets)
## Positioning (quoted headline and messages, source + date)
## Product (capabilities, recent releases, announced-not-available)
## Pricing and packaging (table, with currency, date and caveats)
## Go-to-market
## Customers
## Review themes (theme, n reviews, example quotes, period)
## Hiring signals
## Strengths / weak spots (each with confidence: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW)
## Implications for us (by team, with suggested owner agent)
## Proposed edits to brand/competitors.md
## Sources (all links with access dates)
```

## Quality checks
- Public sources only. No pretext trials, no non-public documents.
- Every fact has a link and date; inference is labelled as such.
- Review themes state their sample size.
- Our own product described only with terms from `brand/glossary.md` and claims from `brand/claims-and-proof.md`.
- Neutral, factual tone throughout.
